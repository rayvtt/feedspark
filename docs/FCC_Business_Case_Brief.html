04 — The team

Three mechanisms, not twenty modules

Each one takes a judgement out of the account manager's head — which is exactly what makes it an onboarding tool.

01

The bar is on the screen

What good looks like used to be learned from whoever had been here longest. 46 rules across the eight free-text fields, each quoting that attribute's own Google specification — so the same feed reads the same whoever opens it.

02

The next move is offered

Knowing what to do next used to be experience. Every brand ranks five next moves from nine deterministic rules, and each one opens its module already filtered to what it just counted.

03

The format is provided

A brief used to be as good as the person writing it. The composer scores six checks ASPL would otherwise come back and ask, and writes the tracking tokens itself.

A new account manager's first fortnight
1

Day one — read

One scoped invite. The dossier per brand: plan, feeds, hours, briefs. Feed questions answered in plain English.

2

Week one — act

First brief scored before it sends. A guard alert worked with the client email already drafted. Go or skip called with the balance in view.

3

Week two — own

Monday call prepared from Leadership. A scorecard exported to the client. A quote on the shared rate card.

And for whoever runs the team: coach from the same screen the AM sees, one standard applied evenly, and a live handover document per brand — so the floor rises while the ceiling is still the account manager.

Said plainly: this shortens the mechanical half of the apprenticeship — where the data lives, what good looks like, what a brief must contain. Not the relational half. Which is why every figure on every screen states its source, and every alert opens down to the products behind it.
05 — Going forward

Ninety days to make it how the team works

Days 0–30 everyone in and a baseline diary measured · 30–60 every brief, quote and deck generated from it · 60–90 the diary re-run, the result reported, the next team chosen. Nothing in the first thirty days needs a developer.
