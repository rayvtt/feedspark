Evidence

Three things nobody could see

Not hypotheticals — each was read off the team’s own systems in the first weeks of the FCC being live.

378 hours in the red

Across 24 markets, carried forward month after month; ten of the 19 retainer markets are in the red. The AM saying yes to the next task could not see it — the Task Manager showed it one client login at a time.

Task Manager, 22 Sep 2026

60% of weeks skipped

In twelve months, 838 of 1,398 scheduled weeks of title, keyword and product-type work were skipped — 2,750 hours of optimisation that never landed. 67 tasks sit on a skip streak of two months or more; the longest is 14.

Content team schedule, 15 Sep 2026

Briefed, but invisible

Two Reiss keyword themes were briefed by a colleague and picked up by ASPL, yet the board read “not briefed” — the save had failed silently and the only record sat on one laptop. The work had started; the team could not see it.

Workflow, 16 Sep 2026

The cost was never a dramatic failure. It was hours of retainer delivered for free, optimisation the client paid for and did not get, and work that existed in one inbox instead of the team's view.

The modules

Monitor, run, sell, lead

Twenty modules, four jobs — each one reading the systems the team already runs.

Monitor — the feeds watch themselves

Label Guard, Product Type Guard, Golden Record, Product Volume, Overlays and Feed Lab scan all 62 feeds four times a day, alert on drop-offs against a known-good baseline, and draft the client email to confirm a change before anyone acts.

Run — the work moves in one place

Workflow takes every task from the plans, the inbox and call notes into one Intake, briefs ASPL with a coded ticket, moves stages from email replies automatically, and shows the retainer balance beside every decision.

Sell — quotes and decks, live

AI Quote prices work per SKU and exports the finance team's own workbook; the Deck Generator builds strategy reviews on the core template; the Keyword Calendar turns a client's marketing plan into briefs and a one-page client PDF.

Lead — the book at a glance

Leadership reads book health, retainer burn-down and a retention radar per account; the Task Manager module makes the hours database searchable; the Playbook tells each AM what is landing, stalling or missing on every account.

Every module reads the systems the team already runs, and writes back to them. FeedHero feeds, the Task Manager, Gmail and the Google Sheets plans — nothing is re-keyed.

Governance

Secure by default, usable from a train

Built to hold client data and to be read on a phone between meetings.

Governed like a client system

Cloudflare Access on every page; each signin scoped to its own clients and modules; every mutation logged per user; client hours kept in Cloudflare KV and never in the code repository; the Task Manager connected through a secret the owner holds.

Built for the phone too

Beyond hours

What the hours model cannot count

The saved hours are the smaller half of the case; the larger half is what stops happening.

A break caught in hours

A custom label or product type that vanishes breaks the Performance Max listing groups built on it. The guards read every feed four times a day against a known-good baseline and draft the client email first.

Overrun as a decision

The 378 hours of negative balance now sit on every task and brand, with the team’s own call — carry the account, or hold new work — recorded beside the number. Overrun stops happening by accident.

Skips seen in time

2,750 skipped hours went unseen because nobody counted streaks. The Schedule module counts them per task and brand, so a three-month skip becomes a client conversation rather than a churn signal found too late.

The larger half of the case is what stops happening.

Vision

What the FCC becomes next

It already reads every system the team runs; next it acts on what it reads.

Feedback — client voice

Sentiment from every client email and call feeding the retention radar; a monthly one-page account report emailed automatically; read-only scorecard links a client can open; a pulse question after each review — churn risk as a number weeks before it is a conversation.

AM management

Workload balanced across the team from the live task book; turnaround and SLA per AM; urgent hours against optimisation hours, tagged and reported; the Playbook as the coaching tool for what each account is missing; a new AM onboarded from the dossier in a day.

Business engine

Revenue forecast from retainer burn, the quote pipeline and new-product volumes; profitability per account; Tachyon drafting the brief and the client email from the finding; fixes pushed into FeedHero and Merchant Center from the screen; feed changes joined to Ads performance; the whole thing packaged for other teams in the network.

The dashboard starts making the first move.

The through-line

Every module that has landed in six weeks does the same thing: it puts the information in front of the account manager at the moment of the decision, so the team's time goes into client work instead of finding out what is going on. The vision is the same idea, one step further — the dashboard starts making the first move.
