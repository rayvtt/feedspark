Chapter three

Unit / tech stack

Four layers, and exactly how much of each is switched on for Monsoon — measured on the feed, not described from the brochure.

Every percentage on this page is read from today's live GB feed. FeedHero writes its own per-SKU processing flag into the feed (c:fs_data_opti), which makes it possible to state what has actually been applied rather than what has been built.

FeedHero — rules & auto-optimised titles

Every SKU in the feed carries a rule-generated title: 8,947 of 8,948 have a populated auto_optimised_title, and FeedHero's own title flag reads 100% processed across the catalogue.

Reach is not the constraint here. Chapter 05 shows what those titles actually measure at.

Custom labels — one slot left empty

Four of five label slots carry real values: CL0 New In (48.9%, 4,380 SKUs), CL2 price status (99.3% — Full Price, Clearance, Promo, Sale), CL3 performance flags (37.7% — Low Performing and Zombie, split Kids/Ladies), CL4 occasion (27.6% — Kids and Ladies Occasion Wear, BTS).

CL1 is 0% populated — and that matches the plan exactly: the open task "Margin data — review matrix for High/Low ROAS vs. Margin — MON CL1" is the slot waiting to be filled. An empty label here is a queued workstream, not an oversight.

Tachyon AI — proven, barely deployed

FeedHero's own flags say how far each AI lane has reached across the live catalogue: keyword enrichment on 410 SKUs (4.6%), description enrichment on 360 (4.0%), image work on 110 (1.2%), and category enrichment on 0.

AI-enriched fields (highlights and details) were signed off as complete in April, and two AI A/B tests are live right now — Monsoon Dresses and Accessorize Bags, full-price only. The engine works; the catalogue has not been run through it yet.

Scrapers & supplemental feeds

Where Monsoon's ecommerce platform does not expose a field, FeedSpark scrapes it. The New In flag behind CL0 is scraped from Monsoon and Accessorize PLPs; a second scrape populates CL4 for social and PPC.

A duplicate feed carrying overlaid imagery runs alongside the main one on the same content_id values, so creative can change without touching the primary catalogue.

Two reads on keyword coverage, and they agree. FeedHero's own processing flag has run against 410 SKUs (4.6%). Counting SKUs that actually carry keyword content in the numbered product-type slots gives 442 (4.9%) — a 32-SKU difference, which is keyword content that arrived by another route. Two independent measurements landing this close corroborate the finding rather than competing with it.

What the stack tells you about this account

Nothing here is missing. Every layer is built, wired and proven on live SKUs — titles at 100%, labels on four slots, AI enrichment tested. The gap on Monsoon is coverage, not capability — a much cheaper problem than the alternative.

Chapter six

Case studies / test wins

Fourteen quantified wins on this account, and one consistent explanation for all of them.

Monsoon's own results, from the project plan's completed test lines — not case studies borrowed from a comparable retailer. Every figure has a named owner and a Done status in the client's own document.

Occasion language moves this catalogue

Ramadan researched keywords returned +86%, Dresses for ladies and kids +61%, Beachwear and Swimwear +27%, Wedding products +24%, Bridal competitor terms +20%.

Monsoon sells occasion. The feed's own CL4 label agrees — 2,126 SKUs tagged Kids Occasion Wear and 301 Ladies Occasion Wear.

Competitor research beats generic expansion

Womenswear competitor keywords returned +39%. The same method on Kids returned +4%. The gap is instructive: the technique is sound, but the return depends heavily on how contested the category already is.

Reported as a range, not an average, because averaging a +39% and a +4% would flatter both.

Steady returns across the everyday categories

Shoes +16%, Kid Outerwear +12%, Ladies Outerwear +11%, Ladies Occasion +9%, Denim +8%, Kid Partywear +4%.

Smaller than the seasonal wins, and consistently positive. No completed keyword test on this account has returned a negative read.

The pattern behind all fourteen

Every win came from putting more specific, more decomposable language into the product data — the occasion, the competitor's term, the category noun the shopper actually types. None came from bidding, budget or campaign structure.

That matters for the agency conversation: these are feed-side wins, achieved without touching media spend, and they compound with whatever the media team does on top.

The same pattern on another account

YuMOVE (pet supplements, UK) ran the same method on a completely different catalogue:

+126.62% "Multivitamins" term
+30% Health-condition terms
+24.7% Benefit-led copy
✓ Brand-in-title

Different category, different price point, same underlying finding: naming the thing the shopper is actually looking for outperforms describing the product.

What 192 hours actually bought

Thirty-two hours a month, across six months, holding 8,948 live SKUs across five channels and two brands — while shipping the New In scrape, the occasion labelling, the sale overlay and the SS26 promotion, and closing fourteen quantified tests.

The figure is contracted retainer time. It is a measure of what the account is resourced to do, not of effort expended — and on this account the resourcing has been enough to run a full test programme alongside BAU.

The work that never appears as a win

A meaningful share of any feed retainer goes on keeping things from breaking, and none of it produces an uplift number:

GMC disapproval sweeps
US availability discrepancy
Site restructure monitoring
Range completion thresholds

Monsoon is mid-way through a sale/clearance URL restructure; FeedSpark monitors product changes through it so the feed does not silently drop lines.

The compounding argument. Every mechanic built in the window — the New In scrape, the occasion labels, the sale overlay, the promotion setup — is a rule, not a one-off edit. It keeps applying to every new SKU that enters the catalogue without further hours. That is why a 32-hour retainer can hold 8,948 live SKUs across five channels and two brands.

Two title tests reached opposite conclusions

The plan carries both: line 13.8, "Run title tests (ladies vs women) — agreed by client → Ladies wins +28% uplift", and line 5, "A/B Title Test [Womens] vs. [Ladies] → Women's wins". Both are marked Done. Only one carries a number.

Most likely they ran in different categories or different windows. Worth settling before either result is used to set a titling rule — it is a quick check against the campaign data, and it decides how every product title in the feed reads.

Plan against the median, not the mean

Two large seasonal reads — Ramadan at +86% and Dresses at +61% — pull the average well above the typical result. The median +14% is the honest number to forecast against; the mean of +24.9% describes the two best months, not a normal one.

No completed keyword test on this account has returned a negative read, which is a genuinely strong record for a programme this size.

Two title tests in the plan contradict each other — line 13.8 "Ladies wins +28%" and line 5 "Women's wins". Both marked Done, only one carries a number. Confirm which is current; if the answer is not quickly available, consider cutting that card rather than showing the client an open contradiction.
