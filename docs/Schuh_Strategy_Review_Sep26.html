P0 gap — highlights are what AI surfaces quote

Titles are 100% rewritten and 2.6% AI-ready

Every UK title in the feed differs from the source title Schuh supplies — the rewrite engine reaches the whole catalogue. 90.5% run through “New Product Name with Size Rules”, the remaining 9.5% are human-reviewed.

What it produces averages 60 characters: “adidas Stadt Trainers in Grey, Size: 8 (EU 42)”. Correct, clean, and about half the 80–120 range AI surfaces reward. Reach is solved; length is not.

Germany is running a different title engine

The UK and Ireland share one: Ireland inherits UK titles on 99.3% of its catalogue. Germany runs “Google product name” on 88.4% instead, and it is the only market where description enrichment has never run at all — 0%, against 100% in the UK.

Germany is not behind on effort; it is on a different pipeline. Bringing it onto the UK engine is a configuration change, not a rebuild.

Where the keywords actually live. Every SKU in every market carries two product_type values: its category path, and a 32-character reference code. Only 0.8% of UK SKUs carry a third, readable keyword value — so the numbered product_type slots are not where this account's keyword work sits. It goes into the title instead, which is what FeedHero's 28.4% flag is counting. Both numbers are right; they describe different fields.

Chapter seven

Case studies / test wins

What the same interventions returned on comparable FeedSpark accounts, and which of them apply here.

Chapter 04 is Schuh's own quantified record. These are the same interventions on comparable FeedSpark accounts — read alongside it, they say which levers Schuh has not tested yet.

Benefit-led copy beat feature-led copy

+24.7% — YuMOVE, pet supplements. Rewriting titles and descriptions around what the product does for the buyer rather than what it is.

Applies directly: Schuh's titles are accurate and descriptive (“Trainers in Grey, Size: 8”) but carry no use-case language at all.

Naming the category term the shopper types

+126.62% — YuMOVE, on the single term “Multivitamins”. Adding the plain category noun a buyer actually searches, rather than the brand's own product name.

Applies with care: Schuh titles already carry a category noun. The equivalent lever here is the second and third noun — occasion, use-case, fit.

Occasion and seasonal language

+86% and +61% — Monsoon, on seasonal and category keyword sets. The largest returns on that account came from occasion terms, not brand terms.

Applies directly: school shoes, wedding, festival and gym are real footwear occasions and Schuh already tags Back-to-School in custom_label_1.

The pattern across all four

Every one came from putting more specific, more decomposable language into the product data — the occasion, the benefit, the category noun the shopper types. None came from bidding, budget or campaign structure.

That is what makes them portable to Schuh: they are feed-side changes, and Schuh's feed is already structurally cleaner than either account these results came from.

What does not transfer, and why that matters

Both accounts started from far weaker attribute coverage than Schuh has. YuMOVE and Monsoon gained partly by fixing gaps Schuh does not have — colour, size and identifiers are already at 100% here.

So these figures point at which lever, not at the size of the return. Schuh's own median in chapter 04 — +14.0% impressions on keyword optimisation — is the number to plan against.

Google conversational attributes

Six attributes built for AI assistants. Schuh UK already submits two of them on every product.

These six fields exist so an AI assistant can answer a shopper's question from the feed rather than guessing from a description. Submission status below is read from the live feeds — present means the field carries real values on that market's products, not that the column exists.

A Schuh customer asks an AI assistant…

Select a question.

Attribute that answers it
AttributeUKGermanyIrelandStatus
item_group_title✓ 100%——Live on all 21,718 UK SKUs — the clean parent name for a variant family
variant_option✓ 100%——Two pairs per UK SKU — Colour and Size, structured name/value
question_and_answer———Not submitted. Highest value and highest governance risk — fit and sizing answers carry return-rate consequences
popularity_rank———Not submitted. Computable from Schuh's own performance data
related_product———Not submitted. The mechanism for footwear-and-care or outfit pairing
document_link———Not submitted. Size guides and care instructions where stable URLs exist

Two live is ahead of most retailers

Both are structural attributes — they describe the catalogue rather than make a claim about a product, so neither needed copy sign-off. That is exactly why they landed first, and it is the same reason the next two should be popularity_rank and related_product.

Extending both to Germany and Ireland is a configuration change on a build that is already proven in production.

⚑ The governance flag — Q&A copy

question_and_answer is the highest-value attribute and the highest-risk one for a footwear retailer. “Do these run true to size?” is the single most-asked question in the category, and a wrong answer at feed scale drives returns.

Proposal: draft from approved PDP copy only, nothing live without Schuh's sign-off. The roadmap keeps structural attributes and claims attributes on separate tracks for this reason.

The work that compounds

Every mechanic built on this account is a rule, not a one-off edit. A product arriving in the feed tomorrow gets a rewritten title, four images, a margin band, a stock band and a variant structure without anyone touching it.

That is what absorbs 2,353 new products a month across three markets without the retainer moving — and it is why the coverage figures in chapter 05 describe batch work, not the automated layer.

The work that never appears as a win

A meaningful share of any feed retainer goes on keeping things from breaking, and none of it produces an uplift number:

GMC disapproval sweeps
Six feeds kept in sync
101 brands of naming variance
Stock & margin re-banding

Three markets, three languages of source data, one set of rules holding them to the same standard.

Nine of eleven workstreams are more than half complete. Keywords sit at 10 of 12 closed, imagery at 8 of 12, optimisation at 11 of 16 and testing at 2 of 2. The lanes furthest behind are product type (2 of 4) and channels (4 of 8) — and both are named in the roadmap, with their dependencies identified.
No hours data exists in Schuh's project plan — no retainer figure, no billable / non-billable split, no time log. The account record carries 46 hours from the original onboarding across UK/IE/DE, which is a setup figure and not a retainer. This chapter therefore states scope and throughput, never hours or cost. Supply the hours and this becomes the strongest chapter in the deck.

Chapter eleven

Spark AI engine (Tachyon)

Intent generation, description enrichment and visual attribute harvest — run against a catalogue that is already 100% image-complete.

01

Feed extraction

Tachyon reads live Schuh product data — title, description, colour, size, material, brand, price.

02

Intent generation

20–40 long-tail intent phrases per SKU, generated from the attributes already in the feed.

03

Scoring & validation

Terms scored against search-term reports; winners selected per SKU.

04

Feed injection

Winning terms embedded in title, description and product highlights — the 90.1% of UK SKUs that carry none today.

Schuh example — Lelli Kelly Sabrina Scarpa

A real SKU from today's UK feed. Input: Black · Patent Leather · Heart Detail · Girls · £60 · School Shoes

Title today (60 chars):
“Lelli Kelly Girls Sabrina Scarpa School Shoes in Black, Heart Detail, Patent Leather, Size: 12”

Intent terms Tachyon would generate:
“patent leather school shoes girls size 12” · “black school shoes with heart detail” · “lelli kelly first day of school shoes” · “scuff resistant girls school shoes”

20–40 generated per SKU, top 4–6 used. The occasion language — school, first day — is exactly the lever that returned the largest gains on comparable accounts in chapter 07.

Why the visual harvest matters most here

Tier 3 normally waits on imagery. Schuh is the rare account where it does not: four images on every one of 43,492 SKUs, with no exceptions in any market.

Vision models read those images for the attributes the feed is thinnest on — material at 17.2% and pattern at 15.7% in the UK, and 7.3% / 4.4% in Ireland. Both are visible in a product photograph.

The raw material for the most differentiated part of the stack is already sitting in the feed, complete, today.

The through-line

Every structural win here — titles rewritten across three markets, identifiers and sizing at 100%, four images per product, margin banded on every UK SKU — is one pattern: fuller, more decomposable data, applied as a rule not a task. Tachyon and the attribute rollout are the next steps on that line.
