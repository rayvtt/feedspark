Chapter nine

The AI Landscape

Reiss shoppers don't only search anymore — they're using LLMs. 

Three pipelines decide whether Reiss is in the answer.

The shift

Search is shifting — from 10 blue links to AI answers

"What should I wear to a summer wedding that isn't too formal?" — a query like this might be answered from training data, a live web crawl, or the Merchant Center feed. The only way to win is rich, accurate, consistent product data across all three.

Feed attributes to enrich for AI readiness
15m
Updates per minute ChatGPT accepts

Zero-click is accelerating

AI overviews and agentic assistants resolve queries without a click. Reiss is either in that answer, or it doesn't exist for that shopper.

The model picks the product

LLMs read structured feeds, attributes and reviews. Titles averaging 53 characters  and 42% highlight coverage mean the model has less to work with than the catalogue could give it.

Fashion is answer-shaped

Fit, occasion, intent — every purchase decision is a question about a specific SKU in a specific size, and that's exactly what these surfaces answer.

Agentic commerce

Three protocols that let AI agents shop on behalf of consumers

Emerging open standards. None requires immediate action from Reiss — 

but they explain why the attribute build in chapters 08–10 matters.

UCP — Universal Commerce Protocol

Google, Shopify, Target, Walmart

Common language for AI commerce
  • Agents handle discounts, loyalty, subscriptions
  • Checkout inside the AI conversation
  • Activated via native_commerce in Merchant Center
MCP — Model Context Protocol

Anthropic, adopted by Shopify

Real-time data access for AI assistants
  • Live inventory, pricing, order status via API
  • Relevant later, once stock-flag accuracy (ch.07) is fixed
ACP — Agentic Commerce Protocol

OpenAI (powers ChatGPT Shopping)

Instant checkout inside ChatGPT
  • Integrates with Stripe, PayPal
  • Currently US-only for checkout; discovery is global

Reiss example — Stanton Petite Wide-Leg Trousers

Attributes: Cream · Cotton · Petite · Size 4 · £58 · sale

AI-generated intent terms:
"petite wide-leg trousers for short women"
"cream corduroy trousers office wear"
"cotton wide-leg trousers under £60"
"petite fit trousers 5ft 3in and under"
// 20-40 generated, top 4-6 used per SKU

Why this matters at 29-market scale (51 markets by Oct/26)

Manually writing intent-rich copy for ~20K SKUs across 29 markets is a scaling question. Tachyon generates the long-tail language at catalogue scale from data that's already 100%-complete on the structural side (colour, material, size, product type, gender).

A Reiss shopper asks an AI assistant…

Attribute that answers it

[document_link]
Points the agent at authoritative fabric/size-guide PDFs. For fashion, this is where full composition and fit-guide detail lives without bloating the title or description.

What this means

None of question_and_answer, item_group_title, variant_option, popularity_rank or related_product  are next on the pipeline to generate & populate in Google Shopping feeds

⚑ The governance flag — Q&A copy

question_and_answer is the highest-value attribute and the highest-risk one for a fashion retailer — fit and sizing answers carry return-rate consequences if wrong.
