The DE/FR content gap GB doesn't have

Six issues sit at or near 100% Red in DE and FR that don't appear on GB's own Red list at all: no lifestyle image, products not human-reviewed, no enhanced data fields, missing optional data fields (all 100% in both DE and FR), plus gender missing from title (DE 85%, FR 87%). Reads as one story — the content-enhancement work behind GB's catalogue hasn't been run on the DE/FR translations at all, not a smaller version of the same gap.

Why this matters for the Local Language case study

This is the concrete, catalogue-level version of the EU keyword-test underperformance flagged in chapter 05 (33% positive vs. 75% in GB) and the reason the Local Language case study exists. Three independent signals — a task-log gap, an underperforming A/B test, and now a full-catalogue content audit — all pointing at the same non-GB markets.

Market-specific

What's unique to each market

Not every Red flag is shared — some are specific enough to be a targeted fix rather than a structural one.

GB — image quality

99% images not square, 98% scene images (not on plain background), 36% missing required data fields. GB's images are live at 100% coverage (chapter 06) but the format itself needs work — not present as a Red flag in DE or FR.

DE — highlights & trash

74% insufficient product highlights — confirms the 1.7-of-10 average fill rate seen in the GB feed at full catalogue scale. 35% flagged as trash products, the highest of the three markets.

FR — category depth

100% of products have a shallow category tree — the widest single gap in this audit. 26% long-materials issue and a 9% duplicate-titles rate (chapter 06) also unique to FR.

Reading three sources together, not picking one: the live GB feed census (chapter 08) says what today's submitted feed actually contains; the A/B archive (chapter 05) says what's been tested and proven; this audit says what FeedHero's own rules catch across the full live catalogue today. Where they overlap (keywords, highlights, Item Group ID), that's the strongest possible signal. Where they diverge (title de-duplication: fixed in GB, still 9% in FR) — that's the useful finding, not a contradiction to resolve away.

Why GB reads 60,345 here and 19,372 in chapter 08: they measure different populations, not different facts. This audit covers the full catalogue; the live Shopping feed carries in-stock items only — every one of its 19,372 rows is availability: in stock, with zero out-of-stock rows, which only happens if the feed is filtered. That puts roughly a third of the GB catalogue in the live feed at any time. ? Worth confirming against FeedHero's own export before it goes in front of the client — the filter is inferred from the feed, not read from FeedHero's config.

6,936 SKUs are live with zero description — and it's not a backlog

Every one of those blank descriptions sits inside the 9,444 SKUs FeedHero's own flag marks as description-optimised — the pipeline believes these are done. 2,508 of that group do carry a real, enriched description (mean 426 characters); the other 6,936 (73% of the "done" bucket) never received the write-back. This isn't old debt: 99.5% of the blank-description SKUs (6,902 of 6,936) were added to the catalogue during 2026 — it's happening on new products right now, not shrinking from a historic backlog.

Undermines the AI-readiness claim in chapter 07

Chapter 07's structured-data readiness table marks name, description, image as TRUE for Reiss' schema.org feed — true in aggregate, but 35.8% of live SKUs are the exception underneath that TRUE. A blank description removes the one field conversational/AI Mode shopping (chapter 09) leans on hardest to summarise a product without inventing detail. Worth fixing before this pattern scales across the 51-market rollout (chapter 14).

Keywords — same gap chapter 07 found, now measured live and slightly worse

94.5% of the live GB feed carries no keyword enrichment today, against chapter 07's audit-tool finding of 89% GB at the time of that full-catalogue scan. Same root cause, two independent measurements agreeing — and moving in the wrong direction rather than closing.

Titles pass FeedHero's own check, but most fall short of the MASK band

FeedHero's Title flag reads 100% pass across all 19,372 SKUs. Measured against FeedSpark's own recommended 80–120 character MASK range, only 1.3% of titles actually land inside it — mean length is 53 characters. Passing FeedHero's check and being AI-ready length are two different bars; this feed clears the first and not yet the second.

Suggested next fixes, in priority order: (1) trace and fix the write-back gap behind the 6,936 blank descriptions — highest priority, since it's live and growing with every new SKU added, not shrinking debt; (2) extend keyword enrichment past its current 5.5% coverage — the same fix chapter 07 already called for, now confirmed live and slightly wider; (3) tighten title length toward the 80–120 character MASK band where category allows. Source: the live GB Shopping feed FeedHero manages for Reiss (refreshes daily) — this read is the 28 Jul 2026 refresh, GB only. The AI Landscape

Reiss shoppers don't only search anymore — they're using LLMs. Three pipelines decide whether Reiss is in the answer.

The shift

Search is shifting — from 10 blue links to AI answers

"What should I wear to a summer wedding that isn't too formal?" — a query like this might be answered from training data, a live web crawl, or the Merchant Center feed. The only way to win is rich, accurate, consistent product data across all three.

Zero-click is accelerating

AI overviews and agentic assistants resolve queries without a click. Reiss is either in that answer, or it doesn't exist for that shopper.

The model picks the product

LLMs read structured feeds, attributes and reviews. Titles averaging 53 characters and 42% highlight coverage mean the model has less to work with than the catalogue could give it.

Fashion is answer-shaped

Fit, occasion, fabric, size availability — every purchase decision is a question about a specific SKU in a specific size, and that's exactly what these surfaces answer.

Agentic commerce

Three protocols that let AI agents shop on behalf of consumers

Emerging open standards. None requires immediate action from Reiss — but they explain why the attribute build in chapters 08–10 matters.

Chapter ten

Spark AI Engine (Tachyon)

Intent generation, description enrichment, visual attribute harvest.

01

Feed extraction

Tachyon reads live Reiss product data — title, description, colour, material, size, price.

02

Intent generation

LLM generates 20–40 long-tail intent phrases per SKU from the real attributes already in the feed.

03

Scoring & validation

Terms scored against search-term reports; winners selected per SKU.

04

Feed injection

Winning terms embedded in title, description, product highlights — closing the product-highlight gap the audit found (58% of SKUs carry none).

Reiss example — Stanton Petite Wide-Leg Trousers

Input (real SKU from the export): Cream · Cotton · Petite · Size 4 · £58 sale

AI-generated intent terms:
"petite wide-leg trousers for short women"
"cream corduroy trousers office wear"
"cotton wide-leg trousers under £60"
"petite fit trousers 5ft 3in and under"
// 20-40 generated, top 4-6 used per SKU

Why this matters at 29-market scale (51 markets by Oct/26)

Manually writing intent-rich copy for ~20K SKUs across 29 markets isn't a resourcing question, it's a scaling question. Tachyon generates the long-tail language at catalogue scale from data that's already 100%-complete on the structural side (colour, material, size, product type, gender).

The title-fix precedent (chapter 05) already proved the AOT pipeline can touch every SKU — Tachyon extends the same reach to descriptions and highlights.

A market that launches Thursday or Friday hits its first real traffic right as the team's attention (and support cover) drops off into the weekend — Mon–Wed means any disapprovals, mapping issues or feed errors get caught and fixed same-week, not discovered Monday after two days live and unwatched.
