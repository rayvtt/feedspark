A row adds that product to Side by side.

  1. Two feeds, one product. The shopping feed is read whole, as Google receives it. The master feed is the client’s own file as FeedHero last imported it. The two are joined product by product on the ID column that carries the most of the feed’s IDs.
  2. Before and after. “Before” is the master feed’s title for that product. Where no master row joins, the original title FeedSpark stamps on each item it rewrites is used and labelled as such. A product with neither has no before and is never counted as rewritten.
  3. Finding an attribute. A title is searched for the product’s own field values first — its brand, colour, material, pattern, size and the last level of its product type — as whole words, singular or plural. Then for values other products in this feed carry in that field. Gender and age group are read from their usual words (women’s, mens, girls, kids, baby).
  4. The mask. The attributes in the order the title says them; words that are none of the eight are the product name. The share of titles on the three biggest masks says how systematic the catalogue is.
  5. The check. For each attribute a title names: the field holds the same value (agree), the field is empty (in the title, not in the field), or the field says something else (differ). Nothing is corrected here — the list is what to fix in the data.
  6. Limits. A word no field in this feed has ever held is read as part of the name. Google’s limit is 150 characters; roughly the first 70 show in a Shopping result.
