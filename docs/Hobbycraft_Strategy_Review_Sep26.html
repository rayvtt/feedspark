Also note the register records 7 live feeds against a maximum of 5 on the current plan; worth a commercial conversation before the next renewal, not a slide.

Seven feeds is unusual for a single-market account

Most accounts this size run two. Hobbycraft runs Shopping, a mapping feed, LIA, Meta, Bing, Pinterest and TikTok off one master feed imported by FTP, on a scheduled refresh — plus four live scrapes topping up web offers daily, product highlights, product type and the back-to-school SKU list.

Every one of those channels is fed by the same rules. A category or label change made once lands in all seven.

Where the next channel is cheapest

The expensive part of a new channel is structured product data, and that is already done: identifiers at 100%, GPC at 100%, 381 brands normalised, 16,720 variant families resolved.

An affiliate feed is a configuration and a QA cycle. So is a second market, if Hobbycraft ever takes the range beyond the UK.

Keywords: the proven lever

Three of the four controlled keyword tests won. Plan against the median: +6.3% impressions and +9.3% clicks, not the single-group headlines.

In both of the wider wins clicks moved further than impressions — the terms are not only adding reach, they are matching intent.

The title question, finally answered

2020's brand-placement test came back inconclusive and the rule stayed "keep [Brand] front". In 2024 brand was removed from Shopping titles; in Sept 2025 it was put back on machines only — +38.69% impressions, +63.63% clicks.

On 40 products, so treat the size of the number with care. The direction is not in doubt, and the live feed now leads with the brand on 94.9% of SKUs.

Christmas measured the season, not the keywords

Both Christmas batches read −46% impressions. Both were single-group tests running 12 Dec to 22 Jan — a window whose second half is the steepest demand drop in the retail year.

With no control group there is nothing to separate the intervention from the calendar. Seasonal ranges need a control, or a window that ends before the season does.

The archive is longer than the current cycle. Eight further results are recorded in the plan from 2020 to 2023, seven of them positive: Valentine's +35%, Digital Cutting +31%, Cricut +22%, Wedding decoration and planning +16%, Sewing +14%, Wedding +7%, Digital Cutting +5% and one early competitor-keyword batch at −8%. Median across those eight: +15%. Six years of testing and the answer has not moved — category-specific keyword work returns high single to low double digits, reliably, and the exceptions are events.
The five "Single group" rows are before/after reads on the whole batch with no control. Three of them (Back to School +50.52%, Black Friday +139.77%, and both Christmas losses) sit in windows with heavy seasonal movement in the same direction as the result. Present them as recorded, but the controlled A/B median is the number to quote in a commercial conversation. Worth agreeing with Hobbycraft that seasonal batches get a control group from Peak 2026 onward.

Three numbers for keyword coverage, and all three are right

The live feed shows a readable keyword string on 50.1% of SKUs. FeedHero's own audit, run this morning against the product database, flags 14,151 products (52%) with no keywords attached — the same answer from a different direction. The feed's process flag reads 26.0%.

The flag counts products touched by a keyword batch; the other two count products that actually carry terms today, including from the category rules. Two independent sources agreeing at roughly half the catalogue is the number to plan against.

Two custom label slots are carrying 2018 and 2020 data

The rule-driven labels are perfect: custom_label_1 (Full Price / On Offer) and custom_label_3 (stock status) are both on 100% of the catalogue. The two that depend on a human upload are not.

custom_label_2 — Best Seller, manual — reaches 0.5% of SKUs, and its values are Bestsellers_Jul20, ChristmasAd_Peak_Oct20, HighBounceRate_Baking_Sept20.
custom_label_4 reaches 3.5%, carrying 2018 Priority Lines and BFCM 2018.

This is not neglect, it is a dependency: the bestseller threshold needs GA access to extract, and that request has been open in the plan since May. Chapter 08 makes it ask number one.

Where the keywords actually live. Optimised keywords on this account are written as additional product_type values, not into the numbered product_type2..10 slots — those read 0% across the whole feed. So a naive saturation check reports zero on a catalogue that is half keyworded. 12,266 SKUs carry a readable second or third value ("simplicity sewing pattern > shop sewing pattern uk", "pink Embroidery Thread > pink Thread"); 11,942 carry only a 32-character reference code. Both readings are correct; they describe different fields.
Highlights need one check before this goes out. The live output feed carries highlights on 45.7% of SKUs (13,278 with none). FeedHero's audit, reading the product database behind it, flags only 5,197 products (19%) with zero highlights. The likely explanation is a feed rule: the scrape has collected highlights for far more products than the Google feed is emitting. If that is right, a large slice of this gap is a rule change rather than new scraping work — which would make W3 in the roadmap cheaper and faster than it currently reads. Confirm before presenting.

The foundation is genuinely strong

Golden Record completeness reads 90.9 against a 99.9% target — and that figure is measured the strict way, scoring each attribute only over the products Google actually asks for it. Material at 47.8% and pattern at 43.8% look thin until you notice there are two clothing products in 24,413, so Google does not ask for either across the range.

Scored the loose way, over every product, the same feed reads 82.7. The 90.9 is the honest one, and it is the higher number for once.

The ceiling, and why it is a ceiling

Google's six conversational attributes are the only fields in the spec whose stated purpose is AI comprehension. None is present in the Hobbycraft feed — confirmed from the schema, not inferred. A feed carrying none of them cannot score above 79.3 on AI readiness however good everything else is.

So the gap is not "some work to do". It is specifically: 0 of 6 conversational attributes, 13,278 SKUs with no highlight, 299 titles in the AI-ready band, and nothing in the category tree reaching five levels.

Craft is the category where this matters most. A shopper buying a sewing pattern, a yarn weight or a Cricut blade is asking a question, not naming a product — will this fit my machine, is this enough for a queen-size quilt, what weight is this yarn. Those answers exist on Hobbycraft's product pages and in the 811-character descriptions already in the feed. What is missing is the structure that lets an assistant quote them rather than guess.

Chapter seven

Case studies / test wins

What the same interventions returned on comparable FeedSpark accounts, and which of them apply here.

The same interventions, on comparable FeedSpark accounts.

Chapter 04 is Hobbycraft's own quantified record — read alongside it, these say which levers Hobbycraft has not tested yet.

Occasion and seasonal language

+86% and +61% — Monsoon, on seasonal and category keyword sets. The largest returns on that account came from occasion terms, not brand terms.

Applies directly, and this is the closest match in the portfolio: Hobbycraft's own trading calendar is a list of occasions — Easter, Halloween, Christmas, Black Friday, gifting, weddings, back to school.

Naming the category term the shopper types

+126.62% — YuMOVE, on the single term "Multivitamins". Adding the plain category noun a buyer actually searches, rather than the brand's own product name.

Applies with force here: 1,236 Hobbycraft titles are under 30 characters and many name the product without naming what it is for — "Clay Beads 68g" is not how a shopper searches for bracelet-making supplies.

Benefit-led copy beat feature-led copy

+24.7% — YuMOVE, pet supplements. Rewriting titles and descriptions around what the product does for the buyer rather than what it is.

Applies to the 13,278 SKUs with no highlights: where highlights do exist here they are already benefit-led ("Easy to thread", "Ideal for bracelet making"), which is why extending them is a coverage job, not a copy project.

The pattern across all three

Every one came from putting more specific, more decomposable language into the product data — the occasion, the benefit, the category noun the shopper types. None came from bidding, budget or campaign structure.

That is what makes them portable: they are feed-side changes, and Hobbycraft's attribute foundation is already stronger than either account these results came from.

What does not transfer, and why that matters

Both accounts started from weaker attribute coverage than Hobbycraft has, and both are far smaller catalogues. Part of their gain came from fixing gaps that do not exist here — identifiers, categories and stock labelling are already complete.

So these figures point at which lever, not at the size of the return. Hobbycraft's own controlled median in chapter 04 — +6.3% impressions per keyword batch — is the number to plan against.

Three things we need from Hobbycraft

Each unblocks work that is already scoped and waiting — none needs new budget.

1. GA access to extract the bestseller threshold. Open since May. It unblocks custom_label_2, which has carried 2020 values ever since.
2. The 2027 trading calendar. Keyword batches need three weeks' lead; the calendar is what lets us be early rather than in-month.
3. An introduction to the new SEO manager. Category depth and the PLP project are the same conversation, and W7 waits on it.

Sequencing logic

W1, W2 and W4 come first because none is blocked on anybody: the keyword pass, the highlight rule and the title engine all run on part of the catalogue already. They are deployment, not development.

W3 and W7 are chased in parallel as client-side dependencies. Everything structural lands in October — once Black Friday opens, nothing architectural moves until January.

Chapter nine

Google conversational attributes

Six attributes built for AI assistants. None is in the Hobbycraft feed today — and two of them need no new content at all.

Six fields an AI assistant can answer a shopper from, rather than guessing from a description.

Submission status is read from the live feed — present means the field carries real values on real products, not that the column exists.

A Hobbycraft customer asks an AI assistant…

Select a question.

Attribute that answers it
AttributeIn the feedContent neededStatus
item_group_title—None — derivedNot submitted. 16,720 variant families already resolved; this names each one cleanly. The cheapest of the six
variant_option—None — derivedNot submitted. Colour is on 85.8% and size on 62.7% of SKUs; this restructures what is there as name/value pairs
popularity_rank—GA / Ads dataNot submitted. Computable from Hobbycraft's own performance data — the same extract W3 needs
related_product—RelationshipsNot submitted. The mechanism for the kit-and-consumable pairing that defines craft retail
document_link—Stable URLsNot submitted. Patterns, instructions and material lists where permanent URLs already exist on site
question_and_answer—Approved copyNot submitted. Highest value and the only one carrying real governance risk — compatibility and quantity answers

Start with the two that need no content

item_group_title and variant_option are structural: they describe the catalogue rather than make a claim about a product, so neither needs copy sign-off and neither can be wrong in a way that costs anything.

Both are built from fields already in the feed. With 16,720 variant families and deep colour and size coverage, this is a configuration change on a build that is proven in production elsewhere in the portfolio.

⚑ The governance flag — Q&A copy

question_and_answer is the highest-value attribute and the highest-risk one for a craft retailer. "Will this fit my machine?" and "is this enough for a queen-size quilt?" are the two most-asked questions in the category, and a wrong answer at feed scale drives returns.

Proposal: draft from approved product-page copy only, nothing live without Hobbycraft's sign-off. The roadmap deliberately keeps structural attributes and claims attributes on separate tracks.

The work that compounds

Almost every mechanic on this account is a rule, not a one-off edit. A product arriving tomorrow gets a brand-led title, a category path 4.18 levels deep, a price band, a stock label and a promotional label without anyone touching it.

That is what absorbs 571 new products a month, across seven feeds, without the retainer moving — and it is why the coverage gaps in chapter 05 are all batch work, never the automated layer.

The work that never appears as a win

A meaningful share of any feed retainer goes on keeping things from breaking, and none of it produces an uplift number:

GMC disapproval sweeps

Twelve disapproval tasks, twelve category-mapping tasks and nineteen client calls in the last twelve months — the housekeeping that keeps 24,464 products approved.

What the feed is carrying commercially. In the last thirty days the Shopping catalogue returned £1,375,637 in conversion value on £127,674 of spend — a ROAS of 1,077% at a £1.54 cost per acquisition, off 53.2 million impressions and 1.08 million clicks. Over the last year the account has spent £1.36 million through the same feeds. Every one of the gaps in chapter 05 sits on top of that base, not instead of it.
The 30-day ROAS block above is FeedHero's own Google Ads read, collected 24 Sep. Do not quote the dashboard's 1-year ROAS of 462% — its annual conversion value (£6.27m) reads low against twelve months at the current rate while annual spend is consistent, which points at the attribution window rather than performance. Spend and impressions are safe at year scale; conversion value is not. Also note 8,139 products took under 100 impressions in thirty days and 3,868 took over 100 with no conversion — the zombie story in W5, and a candidate for its own piece of work.

Chapter eleven

Spark AI engine (Tachyon)

Intent generation, description enrichment and visual attribute harvest — run against 811-character descriptions and a category tree four levels deep.

01

Feed extraction

Tachyon reads live Hobbycraft product data — title, the 811-character description, colour, size, brand, category, price.

02

Intent generation

20–40 long-tail intent phrases per SKU, generated from attributes already in the feed.

03

Scoring & validation

Terms scored against search-term reports; winners selected per SKU.

04

Feed injection

Winning terms embedded in title, description and product highlights — starting with the 13,278 SKUs that carry none.

Hobbycraft example — Pink and Gold Clay Beads

A real SKU from today's feed. Input: Pink · 68g · Jewellery Making > Beads · £5.00 · six highlights, no keywords

Title today (39 characters):
"Hobbycraft Pink and Gold Clay Beads 68g"

Intent terms Tachyon would generate:
"clay beads for bracelet making" · "pink and gold polymer clay beads 68g" · "letter bead bracelet kit supplies" · "friendship bracelet beads for kids"

20–40 generated per SKU, top 4–6 used. Note what the existing highlights already say — "Ideal for bracelet making", "Combine with letter beads, charms and more". The occasion and use-case language is on the product page; it is simply not in the title.

Why the visual harvest matters here

Craft is a visual category where the feed's own words run out fast. 79.7% of SKUs already carry two or more images and 25.9% carry five or more — the raw material for vision models is largely in place.

Vision models read those images for what a craft shopper actually needs: colour accuracy on a 1,000-shade thread range, pattern and finish on fabric, scale on a pack shot. 4,970 SKUs carry only one image, which is where the harvest has least to work with and where the alt-image gap in the audit sits.

The highlight scrape has already shown what enrichment does here: where it has run, SKUs carry five or six well-formed benefit statements. Tachyon extends the same pattern to the half of the catalogue it has not reached.

The through-line

Every structural win on this account — required attributes at 100%, a category tree deeper than most retailers manage, seven feeds off one master, six years of rules still applying — is one pattern: fuller, more decomposable data, applied as a rule not a task. Keyword coverage, highlights and the conversational attributes are the next three steps on that same line.
