Where these numbers come from

  1. The worker reads the FeedSpark reports database itself. Its :15 and :45 cron speaks the reports MCP directly and keeps what it reads in KV — no agent, no export, no manual push. This module has its own rotation on that firing: a couple of markets and a ticket queue each time, stalest first with a market never read always leading, so the whole estate turns over roughly twice a day and nothing starves at the end of the roster.
  2. Nothing here is in the repository. Hours, task titles and client contact addresses are per-client commercial data; they live in KV only. The page is served scoped to whoever opens it, so a client-scoped account manager never receives another AM's rows at all.
  3. All four of the database's reads are here. The client master gives the roster, each market's hours allowance and its balance (Accounts tab); the task list gives every piece of work with its owner, its day and its hours (Tasks tab); the ticket queue gives every client email thread with its age, its idle time and the hours it consumed (Client tickets tab); ticket detail supplies who each thread came from.
  4. Billable and non-billable are never merged. time_taken is charged time, time_taken_nonbill is time we spent and did not charge. Both are real delivered effort, so both count toward what an account cost us — but they answer different questions, so every total on this page carries the split.
  5. The type of work uses the same classifier as the brand one-pager's retainer donut, so the two can never disagree about what a task was. A title matching no rule is Other and is never folded into a category to flatter the mix.
  6. Two traps in the source, both handled in the sync. The database ignores the from_date argument — asking for July returns February — so the twelve-month window is applied when a pull is stored, not by the query. And pulls come back newest-first with a row cap, so a market whose pull does not reach the start of the window is marked partial in the coverage drawer: it contributes the rows it has and says so, rather than a part-year being read as a whole one. A date of 0000-00-00 is counted as undated and never guessed into a month.
  7. Tickets are windowed by their last activity, the same twelve months as the tasks. A thread whose last message predates the window is out of scope for every client alike.
  8. What has not been read is absent, not zero. The line at the top says how many markets the book holds and how stale the oldest read is, and the coverage drawer lists them one by one.
